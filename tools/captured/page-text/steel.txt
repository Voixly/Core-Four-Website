  <h2> What is Stone-Coated Steel Roofing?
  <p> Stone-coated steel roofing is a marvel of modern roofing technology that bridges the gap between industrial strength and residential beauty. It begins with a core of high-tensile, corrosion-resistant steel. This steel is stamped into highly detailed panels designed to mimic the exact contours of traditional luxury roofing materials like Mediterranean clay tile, rustic wood shake, or thick architectural shingles.
  <p> An advanced acrylic base coat is then applied to the steel, followed by a thick layer of natural stone granules, and finished with a clear, UV-resistant overglaze. The result is a roofing system that provides the impenetrable strength, fire resistance, and extreme longevity of metal, but with a beautiful, textured, and traditional aesthetic. For Texas homeowners who want maximum protection against massive hail and severe storms but prefer a classic look over standing seam metal, stone-coated steel is the perfect solution.

  <span.title> Key Benefits
  <h2.title> Why Choose Stone-Coated Steel for Your Texas Home?
  <p.title> Stone-coated steel offers the ultimate compromise between high-end aesthetics and battle-tested durability. It provides all the structural and longevity benefits of a metal roof while dramatically enhancing your home's traditional curb appeal.
  [ICON] fas fa-arrow-right
  <span.button_label> Call us about Stone Coated Steel Shingles
  <h3.title> Unmatched Hail Protection
  <div.desc> Unlike clay or concrete tiles that can shatter, or wood shake that splits upon impact, stone-coated steel boasts a Class 4 impact rating. It absorbs the energy of massive hail, keeping your home protected during severe Texas storms.
  <h3.title> Classic Aesthetic Appeal
  <div.desc> You don't have to sacrifice style for strength. These panels perfectly replicate the rich, dimensional look of traditional clay tile, wood shake, or slate, coming in a variety of natural colors to match your home's architecture.
  <h3.title> Incredibly Lightweight
  <div.desc> Despite its heavy-duty performance, stone-coated steel is one of the lightest roofing materials available (weighing just 1.5 lbs per square foot). It can often be installed without needing expensive structural reinforcements to your roof deck.
  <h3.title> Superior Energy Efficiency
  <div.desc> Because the metal panels are stamped with specific profiles, they create a natural air space above your roof deck. This "above-sheathing ventilation" helps dissipate heat in the summer, significantly lowering your cooling costs.

  <h2.title> Frequently Asked Questions About Stone-Coated Steel
  <div.title> Will the stone granules wash off or fade over time?
  <span.num> 1
  [ICON] icon-plus acc-icon-plus
  [ICON] icon-minus acc-icon-minus
  <div.answer> No. The natural, ceramic-coated stone granules are permanently bonded to the high-tensile steel core using an advanced acrylic polymer layer. They are UV-resistant and designed to retain their vibrant color and texture for decades without washing away in heavy rain.
  <div.title> How does the cost compare to traditional shingles or tile?
  <span.num> 2
  [ICON] icon-plus acc-icon-plus
  [ICON] icon-minus acc-icon-minus
  <div.answer> While the initial investment is higher than standard asphalt shingles, stone-coated steel is often comparable to or less expensive than installing authentic clay tile or slate. When you factor in its 50+ year lifespan, minimal maintenance, and potential insurance premium discounts, it offers incredible long-term value.
  <div.title> Is a stone-coated steel roof loud when it rains?
  <span.num> 3
  [ICON] icon-plus acc-icon-plus
  [ICON] icon-minus acc-icon-minus
  <div.answer> Not at all. The textured surface of the stone granules effectively absorbs and diffuses the sound of rain and hail. When installed properly over a solid roof deck and underlayment, it is just as quiet as an asphalt shingle roof.
  <div.title> Can I walk on a stone-coated steel roof?
  <span.num> 4
  [ICON] icon-plus acc-icon-plus
  [ICON] icon-minus acc-icon-minus
  <div.answer> Yes, you can walk on it, but it requires a bit of care. Because the panels are stamped to have a dimensional profile (like a curve or a ridge), you must step in the designated "valleys" or low points where the metal rests flush against the roof deck to avoid denting the raised sections.
